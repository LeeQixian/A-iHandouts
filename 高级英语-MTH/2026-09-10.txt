这里我们再次强调一下这三类人：他们不是田园牧歌里的牧羊人，不是高贵的野蛮人，也不是平淡乏味的乌托邦主义者（not dulcet shepherds, noble savages, bland utopians）。我再重复一次：他们并不简单。我们自己也一样，并不简单，作者在这里做的是反复强调。

第二类“牧羊人”指的是英国文学中典型的田园生活意象，那个群体喜欢歌唱、喜欢吹笛子，是田园诗里的人物。所以答案并不简单：他们不属于这三类。

“高贵的野蛮人”指的是未开化、没有经过文明污染的形象；“平淡的乌托邦主义者”则是乌托邦作者笔下的那种居民形象。作者说，奥梅拉斯的居民都不是这三种。They were not less complex than us——他们的复杂程度并不比我们低。

The trouble is that we have a bad habit, encouraged by pedants and sophisticates, of considering happiness as something rather stupid. 我们有这样一个坏习惯：受了学究和精于世故者的怂恿，把幸福看成某种愚蠢的东西。

接着看，This is the treason of the artist. 为什么这里会提到艺术家？什么叫艺术家的背叛？treason 的本义是叛国罪，但在这里并不是“叛国”，我们把它翻译成“背弃”。

所谓背叛，指的就是 a refusal to admit the banality of evil and the terrible boredom of pain——拒绝承认邪恶是平庸的，痛苦是乏味的。

也就是说，在这些艺术家、这些写作和从事艺术工作的人眼里，邪恶必须是可怕的、骇人的，痛苦也绝不应该是乏味的；他们笔下的邪恶要有恐怖的面孔，苦难要惊心动魄。

所以接下来是：If you can't lick 'em, join 'em. If it hurts, repeat it. lick 是俚语，意思是打败，既然打不过，那就加入他们；越是痛苦，就越要反复咀嚼。

这里还有一个非常重要的句子：Only pain is intellectual, only evil interesting. 这是格言式的句子，而且用了 parallelism——排比结构，非常有意思。翻译过来很简单：仿佛唯有痛苦才具有智慧、才有思想深度，唯有邪恶才有意思。

也就是说，只有痛苦、只有苦难才被认为有思想深度，只有邪恶才被认为有趣、才配进入作品，这就是艺术家的背叛（this is the treason of the artist）。那么问题就来了：So here's the question: how do you understand the treason of the artist? 你怎么理解“艺术家的背叛”？

And why does he use such a figure instead of other figures? 他为什么一定要这样写，而不是换一种方式来写？

他的意思是：越是痛苦，就越要反复咀嚼；唯有邪恶才有意思，于是就反复歌颂邪恶。但要注意，这并不是说作家就应该去歌颂邪恶。邪恶与痛苦可以是艺术家创作中永恒的主题，只是他们笔下的邪恶与痛苦往往都指向宏大的灾难，而不是我们所说的那种平庸和乏味。

好，我们一起来看这一段：大多数作家，或者说广义上的艺术家，习惯性地书写邪恶与苦难，把它们写得戏剧化，写得骇人听闻、令人麻痹。他称之为“背叛”，因为这样一来，邪恶就不再是平庸的，痛苦也不再是乏味的。

这里的重点在于：邪恶往往是沉默的、单调乏味的，而不是戏剧化的，大家明白这一点吗？

那么第二个问题：他为什么要这样做？艺术家显然有责任去呈现某种真相，拒绝承认它，就是对那种真相的背叛。大家明白这背后的逻辑了吗？

还是要结合前面一句：我们被学究和精于世故者所怂恿，认为幸福是愚蠢的，唯有邪恶才有趣味——仿佛这些才是艺术家们应该去记录、应该去书写的东西。所谓艺术家的背叛，就是说他们拒绝承认邪恶往往是平庸的、痛苦往往是乏味的。

并没有那么多宏大，也没有那么多戏剧性。But to praise despair is to condemn delight, to embrace violence is to lose hold of everything else——赞美绝望，就是对快乐的谴责；拥抱暴力，就等于丢掉了其他的一切。

因为前面提到 If it hurts, repeat it：如果痛，我们就反复咀嚼它，这是很多艺术家所做的事。那么紧接着说，反复咀嚼痛苦就是为绝望唱赞歌，那是对幸福、对快乐的谴责。We have almost lost hold; we can no longer describe a happy man, nor make any celebration of joy——我们几乎已经丢掉了这种能力，再也描述不出一个幸福的人，也无法为快乐唱出任何赞歌了。

How can I tell you about the people of Omelas? 他反反复复在问：我到底该怎么向你讲述奥梅拉斯？

这里用的是 authorial intrusion，也就是作者介入的手法。什么是介入式的作者？就像旁白式的解说，作者直接站出来跟读者对话。可是前面他明明还在叙事，却忽然说 So what do I have to do, how can I tell you?

后面还有多处这样的句子：There were no naive and happy children, though their children were in fact happy——这很好理解。They were mature, intelligent, passionate adults whose lives were not wretched——他们成熟、聪慧、热情，他们的生活并不悲惨。

But I wish I could describe it better. I wish I could convince you. 我希望我能描述得更好，我希望我能说服你，这也是一种（作者介入）。

Omelas sounds in my words like a city in a fairy tale, long ago and far away, once upon a time. 奥梅拉斯听上去像童话里的城市：long ago and far away 是时间上的久远，once upon a time 也是童话式的开头。

Perhaps it would be best if you imagined it as your own fancy bids, assuming it will rise to the occasion, for certainly I cannot suit you all. rise to the occasion 的意思是应付自如；翻译过来就是，最好的做法就是随你怎么想，只要它能 rise to the occasion——它总能达到你的期待值，因为我当然不可能让所有人都满意。

For instance, how about technology? 那么科技会是什么样的呢？他认为这里没有车，也没有直升机。

This follows from the fact that the people of Omelas are happy people. Happiness is based on a just discrimination of what is necessary, what is neither necessary nor destructive, and what is destructive.

也就是说，幸福建立在对这三类事物的区分之上：分清何为必需品，何为非必需且无害之物，何为具有破坏性的，这也是衡量幸福的一个标准（a standard, a criterion for happiness）。

在中间这一类里——非必需但无害的东西，也就是舒适、奢侈、繁盛等等——他们完全可以有 central heating（集中供暖）、subway trains（地铁）、washing machines（洗衣机），以及各种这里还没有发明的奇妙装置：floating light-sources（悬浮光源）、fuelless power（无燃料动力）、a cure for the common cold（感冒药）。What is destructive? 作者提到的破坏性因素，比如 monarchy（君主制）、slavery（奴隶制）、stock exchange（股票交易所）、advertisement（广告）、secret police（秘密警察）等等。

而前面两类我们已经说过：一类是 necessary（必需），一类是 non-destructive（无害）。

这类东西他们当然可以有，也可以完全没有，It doesn't matter, as you like it——无所谓，随你喜欢。所以在科技这一点上，作者的态度其实是很中立的。

接着看：One thing I know there is none of in Omelas is guilt. 我确知奥梅拉斯绝无愧疚、绝无罪疚可言。这里其实是为后文做了一个铺垫。

But what else should there be? I thought at first there were no drugs, but that is puritanical. 我起初想，这里不设毒品，但那样又太清教徒了。仿佛作者是在跟读者商量：要怎样去构建这个奥梅拉斯，除了他说的这些，还需要点什么呢？

毒品肯定是不设的，可是不设又太清教徒了。For those who like it——给那些喜欢它的人：the faint insistent sweetness of drooz，作者在这里自己造了一个词，叫 drooz，非常吻合。

which first brings a great lightness and brilliance to the mind and limbs, and then after some hours a dreamy languor, and wonderful visions at last of the very arcana and inmost secrets of the Universe, as well as exciting the pleasure of sex beyond all belief; and it is not habit-forming. 它能先给身心带来极大的轻盈与光彩，几个小时之后又送来梦幻的慵懒，最后让人窥见宇宙最深处的奥秘，还能带来超乎想象的肉体欢愉，而且它不会成瘾。

再没有什么东西能给人带来这样的轻盈与光彩、这样的梦幻慵懒，这不就是毒品吗？可是作者说，它不是毒品，它不会成瘾——你看好不好，这样的东西还不会上瘾。For more modest tastes, I think there ought to be beer. 口味更温和的人，我觉得就应该有啤酒。

What else belongs in the joyous city? The sense of victory, surely, the celebration of courage. 还有什么属于这座欢乐之城？当然是胜利之感，是对勇气的歌颂。But as we did with our clergy, let us do without soldiers. 但是，就像我们不要教士一样，我们也不需要士兵。

The joy built upon successful slaughter is not the right kind of joy; it will not do; it is fearful and it is trivial. 建立在杀戮之上的快乐，不是正确的那一种快乐，它行不通，既可怕又琐碎（trivial）。

那什么样的快乐才是对的？A boundless and generous contentment, a magnanimous triumph felt not against some outer enemy but in communion with the finest and fairest in the souls of all men everywhere and the splendour of the world's summer. 一种无边的、慷慨的满足，一种宽宏的胜利——它不针对某个外在的敌人，而是与世上所有人灵魂中最美好、最公正的部分相通，与世界之夏的壮丽相通。

This is what swells the hearts of the people of Omelas, and the victory they celebrate is that of life. 正是这些让奥梅拉斯的人们心潮澎湃；他们所庆祝的胜利，是生命的胜利。

还有最后一句我没有放：I really don't think many of them need to take drooz. 我真的不认为他们当中有多少人需要服用 drooz。他们本身就已经足够开心、足够幸福了。

Most of the processions have reached the Green Fields by now. A marvellous smell of cooking goes forth from the red and blue tents of the provisioners. 到这时，大多数游行队伍都已经抵达绿野，一阵美妙的烹调香味从红蓝两色的帐篷里飘出来。

然后是一个细节描写：The faces of small children are amiably sticky. 小孩子们的脸黏糊糊的，却黏得很可爱。

这里要讲一个词：amiably 通常用来描述人，比如 an amiable young lady，一位和蔼可亲的年轻女士；也可以说 in an amiable mood，指心情友善的时候。课文里说的是 the benign grey beard of a man，也就是一位老人慈祥的灰白胡须。

a couple of crumbs——crumbs 是面包的碎屑；entangled，get entangled in，就是被缠住、被纠缠。所以这里说的是，几块酥皮点心的碎屑缠在了他的胡子上，很有趣。

再讲一个词 sticky，黏黏的；benign，慈祥的、良性的。

这一句可以翻译成：小孩子们的脸黏糊糊的，而一位老人慈祥的灰白胡须上还挂着几块酥皮点心的碎屑。

The youths and girls have mounted their horses and are beginning to group around the starting line of the course. 青年男女们已经上了马——mount 我们学过，就是 get on the horses——开始向赛道的起跑线聚集。

他们要干什么？要赛马、赛跑。An old woman, small, fat, and laughing, is passing out flowers from a basket, and tall young men wear her flowers in their shining hair. 一位又矮又胖、笑呵呵的老妇人正从篮子里分发花朵，而那些高个子青年把她的花插在闪亮的头发上。

大家看他们多开心，吃得也好，头发才会这样油亮有光泽；如果吃得不好、营养不良，就不会有这么多（光亮的头发）。A child of nine or ten sits at the edge of the crowd, alone, playing on a wooden flute. 一个九岁、十岁左右的孩子独自坐在人群边缘，吹着一支木笛。

这里要注意，后面还会出现一个年龄相仿的类似的孩子，所以这个人物不是闲笔。

People pause to listen, and they smile, but they do not speak to him, for he never ceases playing and never sees them, his dark eyes wholly rapt in the sweet, thin magic of the tune. 人们停下来听，微笑着，却不去跟他说话，因为他从不停下演奏，也看不见他们——他那黑色的眼睛完全沉醉在曲调甜美而纤细的魔力里。

rapt 这个词非常重要，它的意思是有趣到你不会注意到其他任何东西，一个很常见的用法是 do something with rapt attention，也就是全神贯注地做某事。所以这里说，无论谁与他交谈，他都在吹个不停，也不看他们。

He finishes; and slowly lowers his hands holding the wooden flute. 他吹完了，缓缓放下拿着木笛的手。As if that little private silence were the signal——那片刻私人的静默，仿佛就是一个信号。

All at once a trumpet sounds from the pavilion near the starting line: imperious, melancholy, piercing. 突然，起跑线附近的亭子里响起号角声：威严、忧郁、刺耳。这三个形容词所描写的声音仿佛并不悦耳；而前面描写木笛的声音是 sweet, thin, magic，文中还有多处声音描写，都非常和谐好听，这里的刺耳是在暗示叙事的转向。

The horses rear on their slender legs, and some of them neigh in answer. 马儿用细长的腿腾空立起，其中几匹以嘶鸣回应。

这里还有几个意义相近的词要一起记，有的在第一段里，有的在第五十六页的下面。

Sobered, the young riders stroke the horses' necks and soothe them, whispering, “Quiet, quiet, there my beauty, my hope.” sobered 就是面色严肃凝重下来，年轻的骑手们抚摸着马儿的脖颈安抚它们。

They begin to form in rank along the starting line. The crowds along the racecourse are like a field of grass and flowers in the wind. 他们开始沿着起跑线列队，赛道两旁的人群就像风中起伏的草地与花丛。然后是这一句：Do you believe? Do you accept the festival, the city, the joy? No? Then let me describe one more thing.

这是第二部分起始段，到这里 Part One 就结束了。作者在这里提问：你相信吗？你接受这个节日、这座城市、这份欢乐吗？如果不接受，那就让我再描述一件事。为什么会问接受不接受？因为作者想让读者先表态：等你知道了 one more thing 之后，你还接不接受。接下来就要描述那一件事了。

接下来把这一部分完整地读一遍（课文朗读音频节选）：With a clamour of bells that set the swallows soaring, the Festival of Summer came to the city of Omelas, bright-towered by the sea. The rigging of the boats in harbour sparkled with flags. In the streets between houses with red roofs and painted walls, between old moss-grown gardens and under avenues of trees, past great parks and public buildings, processions moved. Some were decorous: old people in long stiff robes of mauve and grey, grave master workmen, quiet, merry women carrying their babies and chatting as they walked. In other streets the music beat faster, a shimmering of gong and tambourine, and the people went dancing, the procession was a dance. Children dodged in and out, their high cries like the swallows crossing, in and out of the music and the singing. All the processions wound towards the north side of the city, where on the great water-meadow called the Green Fields boys and girls, naked in the bright air, with mud-stained feet and ankles and long, lithe arms, exercised their restive horses before the race. The horses wore no gear at all but a halter without bit. Their manes were braided with streamers of silver, gold, and green. They flared their nostrils and pranced and boasted to one another; they were vastly excited, the horse being the only animal who has adopted our ceremonies as his own. Far off to the north and west the mountains stood up half encircling Omelas on her bay. The air of morning was so clear that the snow still crowning the Eighteen Peaks burned with white-gold fire across the miles of sunlit air, under the dark blue of the sky. There was just enough wind to make the banners that marked the racecourse snap and flutter now and then. In the silence of the broad green meadows one could hear the music winding through the city streets, farther and nearer and ever approaching, a cheerful faint sweetness of the air that from time to time trembled and gathered together and broke out into the great joyous clanging of the bells.

Joyous! How is one to tell about joy? How describe the citizens of Omelas? They were not simple folk, you see, though they were happy. But we do not say the words of cheer much any more. All smiles have become archaic. Given a description such as this one tends to make certain assumptions. Given a description such as this one tends to look next for the King, mounted on a splendid stallion and surrounded by his noble knights, or perhaps in a golden litter borne by great-muscled slaves. But there was no king. They did not use swords, or keep slaves. They were not barbarians. I do not know the rules and laws of their society, but I suspect that they were singularly few. As they did without monarchy and slavery, so they also got on without the stock exchange, the advertisement, the secret police, and the bomb. Yet I repeat that these were not simple folk, not dulcet shepherds, noble savages, bland utopians. They were not less complex than us. The trouble is that we have a bad habit, encouraged by pedants and sophisticates, of considering happiness as something rather stupid. Only pain is intellectual, only evil interesting. This is the treason of the artist: a refusal to admit the banality of evil and the terrible boredom of pain. If you can't lick 'em, join 'em. If it hurts, repeat it. But to praise despair is to condemn delight, to embrace violence is to lose hold of everything else. We have almost lost hold; we can no longer describe a happy man, nor make any celebration of joy. How can I tell you about the people of Omelas? They were not naive and happy children—though their children were in fact happy. They were mature, intelligent, passionate adults whose lives were not wretched. O miracle! But I wish I could describe it better. I wish I could convince you. Omelas sounds in my words like a city in a fairy tale, long ago and far away, once upon a time. Perhaps it would be best if you imagined it as your own fancy bids, assuming it will rise to the occasion, for certainly I cannot suit you all. For instance, how about technology? I think that there would be no cars or helicopters in and above the streets; this follows from the fact that the people of Omelas are happy people. Happiness is based on a just discrimination of what is necessary, what is neither necessary nor destructive, and what is destructive. In the middle category, however—that of the unnecessary but undestructive, that of comfort, luxury, exuberance, etc.—they could perfectly well have central heating, subway trains, washing machines, and all kinds of marvellous devices not yet invented here, floating light-sources, fuelless power, a cure for the common cold.

Or they could have none of that: it doesn't matter. As you like it. One thing I know there is none of in Omelas is guilt. But what else should there be? I thought at first there were no drugs, but that is puritanical. For those who like it, the faint insistent sweetness of drooz, which first brings a great lightness and brilliance to the mind and limbs, and then after some hours a dreamy languor, and wonderful visions at last of the very arcana and inmost secrets of the Universe, as well as exciting the pleasure of sex beyond all belief; and it is not habit-forming. For more modest tastes I think there ought to be beer. What else belongs in the joyous city? The sense of victory, surely, the celebration of courage. But as we did with our clergy, let us do without soldiers. The joy built upon successful slaughter is not the right kind of joy; it will not do; it is fearful and it is trivial. A boundless and generous contentment, a magnanimous triumph felt not against some outer enemy but in communion with the finest and fairest in the souls of all men everywhere and the splendour of the world's summer: this is what swells the hearts of the people of Omelas, and the victory they celebrate is that of life.

I really don't think many of them need to take drooz. Most of the processions have reached the Green Fields by now. A marvellous smell of cooking goes forth from the red and blue tents of the provisioners. The faces of small children are amiably sticky; in the benign grey beard of a man a couple of crumbs of rich pastry are entangled. The youths and girls have mounted their horses and are beginning to group around the starting line of the course. An old woman, small, fat, and laughing, is passing out flowers from a basket, and tall young men wear her flowers in their shining hair. A child of nine or ten sits at the edge of the crowd, alone, playing on a wooden flute. People pause to listen, and they smile, but they do not speak to him, for he never ceases playing and never sees them, his dark eyes wholly rapt in the sweet, thin magic of the tune. He finishes; and slowly lowers his hands holding the wooden flute. As if that little private silence were the signal, all at once a trumpet sounds from the pavilion near the starting line: imperious, melancholy, piercing. The horses rear on their slender legs, and some of them neigh in answer. Sobered, the young riders stroke the horses' necks and soothe them, whispering, “Quiet, quiet, there my beauty, my hope.” They begin to form in rank along the starting line. The crowds along the racecourse are like a field of grass and flowers in the wind. The Festival of Summer has begun.

Do you believe? Do you accept the festival, the city, the joy? No? Then let me describe one more thing. 这一段反复在问同一个问题：你相信吗？你接受吗？那件“另外的事”到底是什么？

In a basement under one of the beautiful public buildings of Omelas, or perhaps in the cellar of one of its spacious private homes, there is a room. 这是一个悖论式的句子：前半句铺垫得很长，主句其实只说了一件事——there is a room，有一个房间。

注意这里的对比：一个是 the beautiful public buildings（漂亮的公共建筑），一个是 the spacious private homes（宽敞的私人住宅），两者构成对照。我们接着看后面是怎样描写的：It has one locked door, and no window. 大家先想象一下这个画面。

这里有个词要注意：A little light seeps in dustily between cracks in the boards. seep 就是渗出，水、空气或者光慢慢渗出来。也就是说，只有一点光带着灰尘，从木板的缝隙里渗进来。

这个房间没有窗户，冬天还潮湿，木板缝里散着灰尘。大家知道，在光线下面，哪怕是仅有一丝光线，那些灰尘都会显得更亮、更明显。这么一个地方，好像不符合奥梅拉斯的美好，为什么会出现这种不合情景的描写呢？接着看：In one corner of the little room, a couple of mops, with stiff, clotted, foul-smelling heads, stand near a rusty bucket.

这里的 mops with stiff, clotted, foul-smelling heads，说的是拖把头又硬又板结，还散发着恶臭。

stiff 这个词我们不是第一次遇到，课文第一段里就有 old people in long stiff robes，那里的 stiff 是形容长袍挺阔、僵硬；这里说拖把头 stiff，就是板结的意思。

旁边是一只生锈的水桶（a rusty bucket），大家在脑海里把这个画面拼出来。接着是：The floor is dirt, a little damp to the touch, as cellar dirt usually is. 地面是泥土，摸上去有点潮，地窖里的泥地通常都是这样，也就难免有很多灰尘。

The room is about three paces long and two wide: a mere broom closet or disused tool room. 这个房间大约三步长、两步宽，不过是一个放扫帚的壁橱，或者一个废弃的工具间，又小又阴暗，没有光，又潮湿又难闻。

In the room a child is sitting. It could be a boy or a girl. It looks about six, but actually is nearly ten. 房间里坐着一个孩子，看不出是男孩还是女孩，看起来大约六岁，其实已经快十岁了。既然连性别都不确定，作者干脆就用 it 来指代他。

用 it 来指代他，其实是一种物化，相当于不把他当人看。

接着看这一句：It is feeble-minded. Perhaps it was born defective, or perhaps it has become imbecile through fear, malnutrition, and neglect. 注意这里给了两种可能：它也许是天生就有缺陷，也许是因为恐惧、营养不良、无人照看才变得痴呆。

It picks its nose and occasionally fumbles vaguely with its toes or genitals. 它抠着鼻子，偶尔含糊地摆弄一下自己的脚趾或其他部位。

as it sits hunched in the corner farthest from the bucket and the two mops. 它蜷缩在离水桶和那两把拖把最远的角落里。It is afraid of the mops. It finds them horrible. 它害怕那些拖把，觉得它们可怕极了。

It shuts its eyes, but it knows the mops are still standing there; and the door is locked; and nobody will come. 它闭上眼睛，可它知道拖把还立在那里，门是锁着的，谁也不会来。

The door is always locked; and nobody ever comes, except that sometimes—the child has no understanding of time or interval—sometimes the door rattles terribly and opens, and a person, or several people, are there. 门总是锁着，从没有人来，只是偶尔——这个孩子对时间、对间隔没有任何概念——门会可怕地嘎吱作响然后打开，门口站着一个人，或者好几个人。

这句话很值得赏析：用词都很简单，但结构讲究。第一个 sometimes 之后，作者突然插入一句解释——the child has no understanding of time or interval。

也就是说，这个孩子对时间没有认知，它并不知道“有时候”到底是什么时候。这里 always 和 sometimes 形成了对比：门永远是锁着的，只是偶尔有人来。

那这个“偶尔”到底是多久？我们也不知道，因为它对时间已经没有感知，可能两天来一次，可能三天，也可能更久。有人来的时候，木门会嘎吱作响，因为那屋子是木头的。

门开了之后：a person, or several people, are there. One of them may come in and kick the child to make it stand up. The others never come close, but peer in at it with frightened, disgusted eyes. 他们中的一个人会进来，踢它一下让它站起来；其他人从不靠近，只是带着惊恐、厌恶的眼神往里看。

The food bowl and the water jug are hastily filled, the door is locked, the eyes disappear. 装着食物的碗和水罐被匆匆填满，门锁上，那双眼睛就消失了。从传统修辞上讲，这里的 eyes 是一个 metonym，也就是转喻，用部分来代替整体。

但在这里，我觉得它更像是一种羞耻，更像黑暗里让孩子极度恐惧的东西——那眼神，因为这些人每次来的时候都是可怕的象征。可能好几天都没有一点动静，然后突然有人来了；屋子里没有光亮，只看见一双发亮的眼睛，接着 THE EYES DISAPPEAR，一切又陷入死寂。

The people at the door never say anything, but the child, who has not always lived in the tool room, and can remember sunlight and its mother's voice, sometimes speaks. “I will be good,” it says. “Please let me out. I will be good!” 门口的人从来不说话，可是这个孩子——它并不是一直住在这个工具间里，它还能记起阳光和母亲的声音——有时候会开口：“我会乖的，请放我出去，我会乖的。”

这太有人性了。所以他究竟是不是智力低下，其实也不好说；甚至可以说，我们宁可他是智力低下，否则对他来说更痛苦。注意这个细节：the child who has not always lived in the tool room——它并不是生来就住在这里。

The child used to scream for help at night, and cry a good deal, but now it only makes a kind of whining, “eh-haa, eh-haa.” 这个孩子从前夜里会大声呼救，哭得很厉害，而现在它只能发出一种呜咽声，而且说得越来越少。

它瘦得腿上没有小腿肚，肚子却凸出来。It is so thin there are no calves to its legs; its belly protrudes; it lives on a half-bowl of cornmeal and grease a day. It is naked. 它一天就靠半碗玉米糊和油脂活着，身上什么也不穿。

Its buttocks and thighs are a mass of festered sores, as it sits in its own excrement continually. 它的臀部和腿上长满了化脓的疮，因为它一直坐在自己的粪便里。读到这里大家可能会想：奥梅拉斯这么美好，难道大家不知道这里关着一个孩子吗？不是的，they all know——每一个奥梅拉斯人都知道。

They all know it is there, all the people of Omelas. Some of them have come to see it, others are content merely to know it is there. They all know that it has to be there. 他们都知道这件事；有些人来亲眼看过，有些人只要知道它在那里就满足了；他们都知道，它必须在那里。

Some of them understand why, and some do not, but they all understand that their happiness, the beauty of their city, the tenderness of their friendships, the health of their children, the wisdom of their scholars, the skill of their makers, even the abundance of their harvest and the kindly weathers of their skies, depend wholly on this child's abominable misery. 有些人明白其中的道理，有些人不明白，但他们都懂得：他们的幸福、城市的美、友谊的温情、孩子的健康、学者的智慧、工匠的技艺，甚至丰收和风调雨顺，全都取决于这个孩子难以言说的苦难。

所以一切——包括更好的天气——全都依赖于这个孩子的苦难。读到这里我们就会想：为什么偏偏是“这一个”孩子？abominable 这个词的意思是极其糟糕、令人憎恶的。

This is usually explained to children when they are between eight and twelve, whenever they seem capable of understanding; and most of those who come to see the child are young people. 这件事通常会在孩子们八到十二岁、似乎能够理解的时候讲给他们听，而来看这个孩子的人大多是年轻人。

前面我们提到，有时候一个人来，有时候好几个人一起看。为什么要这样安排？就是要让这些孩子在能够理解这件事的时候知道它、看见它。可是 no matter how well the matter has been explained to them, these young spectators are always shocked and sickened at the sight.

They feel disgust, which they had thought themselves superior to. They feel anger, outrage, impotence, despite all the explanations. They would like to do something for the child. 他们感到恶心——这种厌恶感，他们原以为自己是可以超脱的；他们感到愤怒、愤慨、无力，尽管一切都已经解释过了。他们想做点什么，为这个孩子做点什么。

大家自然会想：为什么不能改善一下他的生活条件？不能把他救出来吗？不能把那个房间打扫干净吗？都不行。they would like to do something for the child, but there is nothing they can do.

If the child were brought up into the sunlight out of that vile place, if it were cleaned and fed and comforted, that would be a good thing indeed; but if it were done, in that day and hour all the prosperity and beauty and delight of Omelas would wither and be destroyed. 如果把这个孩子从那个肮脏的地方带到阳光下，如果给它清洁、喂饱、安慰，那确实是件好事；可是一旦这么做，奥梅拉斯所有的繁荣、美好和欢乐都会在那一刻枯萎、毁灭。

只要这个孩子不再受苦，哪怕只是被清洁干净、被喂饱、被安慰，只要他得到一点点的善待，所有的美好就会在一瞬间被毁掉。

Those are the terms. To exchange all the goodness and grace of every life in Omelas for that single, small improvement; to throw away the happiness of thousands for the chance of the happiness of one: that would be to let guilt within the walls indeed. 这就是条件：用奥梅拉斯每一个人生命中的美好与恩典，去换取那一点点改善；为了一个人获得幸福的机会，抛弃成千上万人的幸福——那才是真正把罪疚放进了这座城的城墙之内。

条件就是这么严格。这就涉及到伦理学（ethics）的问题了：到底什么是对的、什么是错的、什么是正义的、什么是不道德的。

老师想请大家思考：如果你是奥梅拉斯人，你会不会去救他？看大家陷入了沉思，没有人讨论。有同学说自己会去把他救出来。那我反过来问：这么美好的城市，你为什么不愿意去救他呢？

那请思考一下：在现实生活中是不是有类似的情形？其实这与我们上节课提到的电车难题（trolley problem）很相似。大家都还记得吧：拉动拉杆就能改变电车的方向，但那样会撞死一个人；如果不改变方向，另一条轨道上的几个人都会死。我记得你们当中的大多数人当时选择不改变方向。而到了这个问题上，好像有更多的人选择去救。

但这两者还不太一样：一旦你去救他，全城所有人的美好生活都会随之消失。这难道不也是一种罪吗？你牺牲的不只是你自己的生活，而是全城所有人的生活。

当然，这个故事没有进一步交代（比如那些离开的人到底去了哪里）。如果把它放到现实生活中来看，情形可能更复杂：现实中总是有美好存在，也有痛苦存在。

大家还记得上节课讲的，这类作品的一个特点，就是引导读者去思考那些我们习以为常、认为理所当然的问题。

也就是说，通过这种科幻式的情境，你不得不去思考：刚才有四五位同学选择去救他，可是你很可能因此遭到更多人的谴责，因为你实际上破坏了所有人的美好生活，而换来的可能只是这一个孩子的处境稍稍改善了一点点。你愿意吗？如果你认为应该救，那你坚持的是某一种道理。

我相信肯定也有同学不愿意去救：因为那样会造成更多人的不幸，这确实是一种罪恶。那么，在这种情形下，坚持另一种选择的人所依据的道理又是什么呢？

所以，凡是按“最多数人的最大幸福”来做的选择，都属于一种后果论（consequence）——根据结果来衡量得失，再决定我是否要那样做。好，今天的课就上到这里。课间给大家安排一个小作业：用 AI 去生成这个孩子的生活，做一个小视频。
